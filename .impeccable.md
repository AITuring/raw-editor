## Design Context

### Users

Personal photographers using a local, non-destructive RAW workflow, primarily on macOS. They need to move quickly from import through selection, global adjustments, detailed inspection, and export, including large 60 MP files. The core editing model and terminology should feel familiar to people who already understand Adobe Camera Raw, without copying Adobe's interface pixel for pixel.

### Brand Personality

Professional, restrained, and dependable. The interface should create confidence that the photograph is visible, the current processing state is understandable, and every adjustment is reversible. It should feel like a focused photographic instrument rather than an AI product, social product, or decorative design showcase.

### Aesthetic Direction

A dense but calm desktop editing workspace with clear tonal hierarchy, precise alignment, compact controls, and neutral image-surround colors. Camera Raw is a workflow and information-semantics reference; RAW Editor keeps its own warm accent and flatter, quieter visual language. Both light and dark themes are supported. Avoid generic card-heavy layouts, ornamental gradients, oversized rounded controls, and color-only communication.

### Design Principles

1. The photograph is always the primary object: never replace a usable preview with an empty canvas during loading or renderer handoff.
2. Make photographic meaning immediately legible: analytics use familiar left-to-right shadow/midtone/highlight semantics, overlaid RGB channels, and explicit clipping cues.
3. Prefer continuity over spectacle: preserve the previous or thumbnail frame until the next valid frame is demonstrably ready; motion only explains state transitions.
4. Keep expert workflows compact and predictable while providing labels, tooltips, keyboard focus, and non-color cues where meaning could otherwise be ambiguous.
5. Preserve local-first trust: loading, failure, fallback, and renderer state should be visible and recoverable without obscuring the image or losing edits.
